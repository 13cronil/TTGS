# Three-Day Climbing-Day Programme (v2)
### Hypermobile climber: elbow control, wrist/shoulder durability, pull strength, fingers, L-sit, pistol

---

## 0. Before starting

One in-person assessment by a climbing-literate physio. The purpose is to confirm the elbow is a *motor-control* problem (passive lockout, poor position sense) rather than a specific tendon or ligament lesion — everything below assumes the first and is wrong if it's the second.

Week 1: Beighton score (≥5/9 in adults suggests generalised hypermobility; a low score doesn't exclude local laxity), plus a knee-to-wall ankle measurement each side and a note of any history of subluxation, painful clicking, or slow recovery.

---

## 1. Assumed climbing structure

The supplementary work is sized around what the climbing actually is. This plan assumes:

- **Day 1 — Limit bouldering.** Hardest session, highest finger load, most neurally fatiguing.
- **Day 2 — Volume / technique.** Sub-limit mileage, moderate finger load.
- **Day 3 — Routes, projecting or mixed.** Moderate intensity, longer duration.

Spacing ≥48 h, e.g. Mon / Wed / Sat. **If the real sessions differ, the finger protocol and the post-climb allocation both move** — see §3 and §10.

---

## 2. Design logic

**Strengthen, don't stretch.** Lax ligaments can't be tightened; muscle is the only secondary brace available. No static stretching of elbow, lumbar spine or shoulder. Stretching genuinely tight tissue elsewhere (hips, calves, pecs) is fine.

**Load hard anyway.** Shoulder-MOBILEX (Liaghat, *BJSM* 2022, n=100 HSD/hEDS) found heavy full-range strengthening superior to gentle low-load work at 16 weeks, no serious adverse events, and slightly *fewer* instability events in the heavy group. A 12-week self-guided low-intensity RCT in hypermobile women produced no strength gains at all. Light-band purgatory is the failure mode to avoid.

**The deficit is position sense, not force.** Akaras et al. (*Sci Rep* 2025, n=83): hypermobile adults had significantly poorer elbow and knee proprioception with no difference in grip strength or functional performance. Hence tempo control, closed-chain work, and co-contraction drills alongside the heavy lifts.

**The elbow fault is "false stability."** Snapping into hyperextension feels stable but switches off the surrounding musculature. It is not press-ups that are the problem — it is terminal extension under load. Same error shows up in a passive dead hang and a collapsed pistol bottom, so the same cue applies in all three.

**Connective tissue runs slower than muscle.** Hold each load level 2–3 weeks. Gate progression on the 24-hour response, not on how the set felt.

**Fingers are capped by tissue recovery.** Three climbing days already means three finger-loading days. Consensus (López, Hörst, Nelson) is 2–3 genuinely hard finger sessions weekly with ≥48 h between, and limit bouldering counts as one.

**Time is a constraint, not a detail.** A plan that isn't finished doesn't work. Everything below is supersetted and audited; if a session routinely overruns, cut the last item rather than extending.

## 3. Weekly map and time audit

| | **Day 1 — Limit boulder** | **Day 2 — Volume/technique** | **Day 3 — Routes/mixed** |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Pre-climb** | 10 min prep + 4 min finger primer | 10 min prep + 14 min full block protocol | 10 min prep + 5 min low-intensity fingers |
| **Post-climb** | Legs + trunk — **30 min** | Pull + elbow + wrist — **34 min** | Horizontal pull + shoulder + wrist + trunk — **36 min** |

**The finger-load collision, decided in advance:** the full block protocol goes on **Day 2**, where the climbing that follows is sub-limit. Day 1's climbing *is* the hard finger session, so the block work that day is a 3-set primer at ~70%, not a training stimulus. Block pulls at maximum intensity and limit bouldering never share a day.

This yields two hard finger days per week (Day 1 via climbing, Day 2 via the block) with ≥48 h between.

---

## 4. Pre-climb block — every session, 10 minutes

1. **Pulse raise** — 3 min.
2. **Shoulder** — band ER 1×15 light, scapular pull-ups 1×5, wall slide 1×8. *Spanhove & De Wandele (Physiotherapy 2020): in hypermobile shoulders, isometric ER produces a stabilising inferior humeral-head glide, whereas flexion and extension drive it anterior-superior.*
3. **Wrist** — **unloaded** banded rock-backs, 10 each direction. Nothing loaded. Loaded wrist work goes after climbing; doing it first costs grip for the session.
4. **Elbow co-contraction** — hand flat on a bench, elbow 5–10° short of end range, co-contract biceps *and* triceps, **2×20 s per arm**. Progress by shifting bodyweight around the fixed hand while holding.
5. **Finger warm-up** — 4 progressively heavier pulls, open-hand, well short of limit.

**Graduation criterion for the co-contraction drill:** when he can hold 30 s with bodyweight shifted over the hand, no snapping, on both arms, for two consecutive weeks — *and* Stage 2 elbow work is running — drop to 1×20 s as a pre-climb primer. Retire it entirely once Stage 3 is clean, and reinstate only if symptoms return. A motor-control drill unchanged at week 20 has failed.

---

## 5. Finger protocol

**Use a lifting block/pin rather than a hangboard.** For lax tissue the block wins on load precision, joint-position control, no swing or shear, no shoulder/elbow load, and grip reset between reps. The hangboard's advantage is climbing-chain specificity — worth one block of hangboard work per cycle, but not the default.

- **Day 2 (full protocol, ~14 min):** 20 mm edge, half-crimp, arm at side. **5 × 10 s per hand**, alternating hands, ~3 min per hand.
- **Day 1 (primer, ~4 min):** 3 × 8 s at roughly 70% of Day 2's load. Warm-up quality, not stimulus.
- **Day 3 (~5 min):** low-intensity sustained loading, 2 × 2 min at roughly 40% of max, feet taking the difference.

**Intensity is governed by margin, not by the calendar.** Stop with roughly 3 s left in the tank (López). Never to failure — this matters more with lax tissue than with normal tissue.

**Load only follows margin:** when 5 × 10 s at the current load consistently leaves 4+ s in reserve, add weight. No fixed weekly increment — linear micro-loading on a block will stall inside a month for anyone with training age, and forcing it is how joint-position control degrades.

**Grip bias.** Hypermobile climbers over-crimp because full crimp *feels* stable, but it couples ~90° PIP flexion with DIP hyperextension — the position that maximises A2 pulley strain (A2 bears 3–4× fingertip force, 380–700 N in recreational climbers; Miro/Schöffl 2021). Keep the great majority of volume in half-crimp and open-hand. Full crimp only on confident holds, low volume, never on the block.

Alternate grip emphasis between cycles: half-crimp → open-hand / 3-finger drag.

## 6. Day 1 post-climb — Legs and trunk (30 min)

Deliberately no pulling. After limit bouldering the nervous system and the fingers are the limiting resources; this is the cheapest session to place here.

**A1. Bulgarian split squat — 4 × 6 each leg.** This is the strength base for the pistol and it is programmed, not optional.
**A2. Ankle** *(superset)* — heel-elevated squat holds, or knee-to-wall mobilisations 10 each side if the measurement was restricted. Dorsiflexion *range* is the usual pistol limiter; train range or elevate the heel, don't try to solve it with dorsiflexor strength work.

**B. Pistol ladder — 3 × 5 each leg.** Box squat to a fixed height, 3 s descent. Stop at the bottom and own it actively — don't sink into the passive bottom position. Same principle as the elbow.

**C. Seated pike compression lift — 3 × 5**, 3 s holds. Legs straight, hands on blocks beside the hips, actively lift the heels. This is the primary driver of the L-sit; the pike is the limiter, not the hang.

**D. Parallette tuck L-sit — 3 × 20 s.**

**Hollow hold is a screen, not a set.** Before the compression work, hold hollow for 20 s. If the lumbar spine won't stay flat against the floor, replace C and D that day with dead bugs 3 × 8/side until it will. Once the position is reliable for two weeks, drop the screen.

---

## 7. Day 2 post-climb — Pull, elbow, wrist (34 min)

**A1. Weighted pull-up — 5 × 3–5**, 2–3 min rest.
- Not yet loading: 5 × 5 strict bodyweight, then add weight.
- **Active bottom position** — elbows soft, scapulae engaged. No passive dead hang under load; that's the same false-stability pattern as the locked elbow. Short unloaded hangs in warm-up are fine.

**A2. Eccentric biceps brake — 3 × 8** *(in the rest periods)*, 4 s lowering, stopping 10–15° short of full extension. Supinated curl. This is the direct treatment for hyperextension: training the biceps to decelerate the joint before it reaches the stop (Kaux 2013; BraceLab protocol).

**B1. DB floor press — 3 × 8.** The floor caps the bottom range and there's no bodyweight-driven terminal lockout. Stop short of straight; keep the triceps switched on at the top.
**B2. Side-lying external rotation — 3 × 12** *(superset)*.

**C. Wrist battery — 2 rounds, 2–3 RIR** (all six actions, same battery both days it appears):
- Wrist flexion + wrist extension, 10 each
- Pronation + supination with a lever, 10 each
- Radial + ulnar deviation with a lever, 10 each

Deviation is trained **directly with a lever**, not inferred from hammer curls and triceps extensions. Ulnar-sided wrist problems (TFCC, ECU) are among the two dominant overuse presentations in climbers' wrists; if it's on the risk list, train it as a named action.

---

## 8. Day 3 post-climb — Horizontal pull, shoulder, wrist, trunk (36 min)

**A. Barbell or ring row — 4 × 8.** The primary horizontal pull, and the largest gap in the original plan. A climber with an anterior-dominant shoulder and a symptomatic elbow gets more from horizontal pull volume than from a second near-max vertical pull day — high-volume vertical pulling on top of three climbing days is a common route into medial elbow problems.

**B. Pull-up — 3 × 5 at RPE 7.** Frequency without the volume. Deliberately well short of failure; this exists to keep the pattern grooved between heavy days, not to add fatigue.

**C. Shoulder block — 3 × 10, supersetted** (~10 min): prone horizontal abduction · prone ER at 90° abduction · seated scaption in the scapular plane (20–30° forward of frontal). Controlled tempo, ~1 s up / 2 s down. No ballistic reps.

**D. Wrist battery** — as §7C.

**E. Hanging raise progression — 3 × 8.** Tuck → advanced tuck → single-leg extended → straight-leg. Deliberate posterior pelvic tilt to start each rep, no swing. If the tilt disappears, the set is over.

---

## 9. Pressing and the elbow — staged

Dips have been removed from the main programme. The justification for prioritising external rotation is that loaded shoulder *extension* drives the humeral head anterior-superior in lax shoulders — and a dip, especially a bench dip, is exactly loaded shoulder extension near end range. Capping the *elbow* angle doesn't address that. The pressing progression runs through range-limited horizontal pressing instead.

- **Stage 1 (weeks 0–4):** co-contraction holds, mid-range isometrics (90° and partial extension), wall and high-incline presses stopped well short of lockout. *Gate: pain-free co-contraction holds; incline pressing with no hyperextension.*
- **Stage 2 (weeks ~4–10):** eccentric biceps brake, slow triceps extensions, DB floor press. *Gate: floor pressing with no elbow pain and no snapping; clean 30 s weight-shifted co-contraction.*
- **Stage 3 (weeks ~10+):** parallette or deficit push-ups, then standard push-ups holding a muscular micro-bend at the top. *Gate: 3 × 8 clean with no terminal collapse.*
- **Stage 4 — loading terminal extension (optional, see below).**

### Stage 4: the end-range bridge

The plan's own logic concedes that permanent micro-bend is an interim measure, not an endpoint — the aim is *choice and control* over end range, not lifelong avoidance. Nothing in Stages 1–3 moves toward that, so:

1. Banded press-downs finishing at full extension against light resistance, held 3 s — actively extended, never passively locked.
2. Straight-arm support holds on parallettes, hands turned slightly out, 3 × 20 s, elbows fully extended and *actively* held.
3. Weight shifting over the fully extended supporting arm, progressing load rather than range.
4. Only then, if relevant: low, shoulder-capped dip support holds, humerus never travelling behind the torso.

Rule throughout: **load at end range must be actively held, never hung on.** Progress by load, not by pushing past normal end range.

**Whether to run Stage 4 at all depends on the goal.** If topping out and mantling matter, the elbow eventually has to be strong at end range and this bridge is necessary. If the pressing objective was only "press-ups hurt," Stage 3 is a legitimate endpoint. This is also the thinnest-evidence section in the document — there is no trial data on progressive end-range loading in hypermobile elbows, only the clinical argument that avoidance doesn't build tolerance.

## 10. Progression, monitoring, deload

**Single programme-wide cycle: 4 weeks of progression, then one deload week at ~70% of loads, same movements.** This applies to fingers, lifts and durability work together — no separate cadences to track.

**The 24-hour rule gates everything.** Pain or stiffness that settles to baseline by next morning and is stable or decreasing = progress. Pain rising with added load = regress one level, reassess in 1–2 weeks. Low stable pain is acceptable; rising pain is not.

| | Progress when | Regress when |
|---|---|---|
| **Fingers** | 5 × 10 s leaves 4+ s in reserve | Finger *joint* pain >48 h, or climbing performance drops |
| **Pull-ups** | Top set hits 5 clean reps with an active bottom → +2.5 kg | Elbows snap straight at the bottom |
| **Rows** | 4 × 8 clean → add load | — |
| **Elbow** | Stage gates in §9 | Snapping back into extension, post-session ache, or defaulting to passive lockout |
| **Wrists** | Hold a load 2–3 weeks, then add; load before reps | 24-h rule |
| **Shoulder** | Borrow MOBILEX's logic — 3 weeks familiarisation at increasing % of 10RM, then 3 × 10RM, then 4 × 8RM, then taper. **Not its numbers:** the trial's 15 kg was a dumbbell ceiling across five exercises, not a target for side-lying ER | Apprehension or instability → back to isometric ER and closed-chain weight-bearing before open-chain overhead |
| **L-sit** | 3 × 20 s clean at the current stage, no lumbar collapse | Tilt lost, or spine collapsing into flexion |
| **Pistol** | 3 × 5 clean per leg, 3 s descent, no bottom collapse → drop box ~5 cm | Knee drifting in, or resting passively at the bottom |

**Lumbar note:** train active compression, not passive pike stretching. Avoid loaded end-range spinal flexion (Jefferson curls and similar). If hamstring length limits the pike, build it with loaded long-range work such as RDLs. Clinical reasoning, not trial evidence.

## 11. Evidence ledger

**Strong (RCT / controlled trial).** Heavy full-range shoulder strengthening beats low-load in hypermobility short-term, safe, no serious adverse events (Liaghat, *BJSM* 2022) — though by 1 year both groups had improved and the heavy group was no longer statistically superior, so adherence matters as much as intensity. Proprioceptive closed-chain training improves position sense, pain and function in joint hypermobility syndrome (Ferrell, *Arthritis & Rheum* 2004). Poorer elbow/knee proprioception with preserved grip strength in hypermobility (Akaras, *Sci Rep* 2025). Max hangs produce the largest peak finger-strength gains, repeaters better for endurance (López-Rivera & González-Badillo). Heavy slow resistance ≈ eccentric protocols for tendon, better adherence (Kongsgaard/Malliaras).

**Moderate (case series, retrospective, mechanistic).** Supervised heavy resistance tolerated and beneficial in hypermobile knees — pain −32.5 mm VAS, 5RM +39–82%, proprioception error −55% (Henriksen 2022; uncontrolled, n=16, knee). Low-intensity finger loading matching max hangs and combining additively (Gilmore/Baar 2024; retrospective, self-reported loads). Eccentric training for elbow hypermobility (Kaux 2013; single case report). Baar's short-bout collagen model (in-vitro engineered ligaments, no large human trials).

**Thin — clinical reasoning only.** Everything L-sit- and pistol-specific: there is no meaningful literature on gymnastic compression or single-leg squat progressions in hypermobile populations, and these ladders are gymnastics coaching filtered through the principles above. The lumbar recommendation. Stage 4 end-range loading. Climbing-specific hypermobility injury data barely exists; most inference is borrowed from dance, netball and general EDS cohorts, which themselves conflict.

---

## 12. Stop and get it looked at

- Sharp elbow joint-line pain, swelling, painful clicking, or the elbow giving way
- Tingling in the ring/little finger (possible ulnar involvement)
- Any true subluxation or dislocation
- Shoulder night pain, or catching/locking (possible labral)
- Ulnar-sided wrist pain limiting weight-bearing (possible TFCC)

Not train-through problems.

---

*Not medical advice. Written assuming generalised joint hypermobility without a formal hEDS/HSD diagnosis; if a formal diagnosis exists or subluxations are frequent, individual assessment takes precedence.*
