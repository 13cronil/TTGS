Two days a week

Time to get
Strong!

Everything bolted onto days you're climbing anyway. About fifteen minutes before, forty after. Tap any exercise to see the movement.

The whole thing

1

Do this twice a week, on days you're already climbing. Leave at least two days between them.

2

Before you climb — 15 min. Warm up properly, then block pulls while you're fresh — edges one session, pinch the next.

3

After you climb — 40 min. Seven exercises, in the order shown below. Fit the face pulls into the rests between press sets.

4

On every set, stop with two reps left in you. Never grind out the last one.

5

When the last set felt easy, add a little weight next time. That is the entire progression.

6

Every fifth week, go light. Same exercises, roughly two thirds of the weight.

Nothing here needs a gym beyond a bar, some dumbbells and a band. Swaps are listed on each exercise.

Pull-ups→Press→Face pulls→Curls→Leg lifts→L-sit→Wrists

Before you climb

15 min

Wrist rock-backs. Hands flat, fingers pointing forward. Rock forward and back ten times, then turn the hands to point at your knees and repeat.

  1. Two minutes of anything that raises your pulse and warms the hands — skipping, rowing, arm swings, jogging on the spot.
  2. Rock-backs both directions, as above.
  3. Open and close the hands hard twenty times, then shake them out.
  4. Four or five easy climbs on big holds before anything hard.
WhyFinger pulleys take the highest load of any tissue in climbing. Two minutes of warm-up is the cheapest injury insurance available.

Four holds each hand. Holds 1 and 2 in half crimp, holds 3 and 4 in full crimp.

  1. Half crimp: fingertips on the edge, middle knuckles bent to about 90°, thumb off the edge.
  2. Full crimp: middle knuckles bent past 90°, fingertip joints pushed back, thumb wrapped over the index finger.
CareFull crimp puts the most load on the finger pulleys of any grip. Start it lighter than your half-crimp weight, and drop it for the day if a finger feels tweaky.

No block or pin? A 20mm hangboard edge with your feet on a chair, taking enough weight through the legs that ten seconds is hard but controlled.

Four holds each hand. Thumb flat on one face, fingers on the other, wrist held slightly back.

No pinch block? A bumper plate or a thick, smooth-sided book lifted by its edge.

Both sessions

  1. Ramp up first. Three easy lifts at roughly 40%, 60% and 80% of your working weight, five seconds each, in the grip you're about to train.
  2. Stand tall, arm straight by your side, shoulder pulled down. Lift the weight just clear of the floor.
  3. Hold ten seconds, put it down, swap hands. Three minutes between holds on the same hand — the other hand's hold goes in the middle of that rest. Four rounds each side.
LoadPick a weight where ten seconds ends with about three seconds still in you. Never to failure — this is the one place where pushing to the limit reliably costs you a finger injury.

After you climb

40 min

Up in one second, down in two. Two to three minutes between sets — long rests are what make this a strength lift.

  1. Hang with your hands a little wider than your shoulders.
  2. Set the shoulders first. Pull them down away from your ears before the arms do anything. Everything starts here.
  3. Pull your chest toward the bar, chin clearly over it.
  4. Lower over two slow seconds and keep a little tension at the bottom.
Session A5 × 3–5 heavy
Session B4 × 6–8 easy
ProgressWhen session A's top set hits five clean reps, add 2.5 kg in a belt or a rucksack. Can't do five yet? Jump to the top and take five seconds to lower — five of those, three sets.

Up in one second, down in two. Two minutes between sets.

  1. Lie on a bench, dumbbells at chest height, elbows at about 45° to your body — not flared straight out to the sides.
  2. Press up and slightly together until the arms are straight but not jammed.
  3. Lower over two seconds until your upper arms are level with your ribs, then press again.
CueShoulder blades pinned down and back into the bench throughout. If they roll forward at the top you've reached too far.

No bench? Do it on the floor. The floor stops your elbows at a sensible depth for you.

Pause one second at the back. Light weight. One minute between sets.

  1. Band or cable at roughly head height. Step back until the arms are straight and the band has tension.
  2. Pull toward your forehead, leading with the elbows. Elbows stay level with your shoulders, not below.
  3. Hold a second with your hands either side of your head, then let it back out slowly.
CueIf the elbows drop it's become a row and stopped doing the job. Go lighter before you let that happen.

No band or cable? Lie face down on a bench and make the same shape with 2–4 kg dumbbells.

One second up, four seconds down. The slow lowering is the entire point.

  1. Stand tall, dumbbell in each hand, palms forward, elbows pinned to your sides.
  2. Curl up in one second.
  3. Take four seconds to lower and stop just before the arm is fully straight.
WhySlow lowering builds an elbow that can control load, not just produce it. Go lighter than your ego wants — the tempo is what does the work.

Lift, hold three seconds, lower slowly. Five reps, three rounds.

  1. Sit on the floor, legs straight, hands flat beside your hips — on blocks or books if that's easier to reach.
  2. Sit up tall first. Chest up, back long, shoulders pushed down.
  3. Lift both heels a few centimetres and hold three seconds. Lower under control.
CueIf your back rounds to get the legs up, the rep doesn't count. Lift less height and keep the chest tall. That's the whole exercise.

Hanging knee raise — 3 × 8. No swing. If you start using momentum, the set is finished.

Tuck hold — 3 × 20s, then extend one leg at a time.

  1. Knee raises: hang, tilt the pelvis so the low back flattens, then bring the knees to hip height and lower slowly.
  2. Tuck hold: on parallettes or two chairs, press down hard and drive the shoulders away from your ears. Knees tucked, feet clear of the floor.
  3. Once three clean twenty-second holds feel steady, straighten one leg. Then the other. Both legs is the last step, not the first.
CueArms dead straight, shoulders pushed down, chest up. If the shoulders creep toward your ears, come down and reset rather than fighting it.

Rest your forearm along your thigh or a bench with the hand hanging off the end. Work through all four, then repeat the round. Two or three reps left in the tank on each — these are small muscles and they bruise easily.

1 · Wrist curl — palm up, 10–15 reps. Let the weight roll to the fingertips, then curl it back.

2 · Reverse curl — palm down, 10–15 reps. Much lighter than you expect.

3 · Rotation — 10–15 slow reps. Hold the dumbbell at one end only, or use a hammer.

4 · Tilt — 10–15 reps each way. Small range, slow, wrist only.

KitOne light dumbbell covers all four. No dumbbell? A hammer, a loading pin, a 1-litre bottle or a large tin all work — hold them at one end for movements 3 and 4.

When to add weight

Add

The last set felt like you had two or three more reps in you. Add the smallest jump you have — 1 kg on fingers and wrists, 2.5 kg elsewhere.

Add

You hit the top of the rep range on every set, two sessions running.

Hold

You had to swing, bounce or lose the shape to finish. Stay here until it's clean.

Hold

Something is still sore the next morning. Repeat the week as it was, or drop a set.

Back off

Soreness that gets worse as you add weight, or joint pain lasting more than a couple of days. Return to the last comfortable weight and rebuild from there.

Four weeks of building, then a fifth week at roughly two thirds of the weight with the same exercises. Start the next block a little above where you finished the last one.

What this is based on

Twice a week is enough. With total weekly sets matched, training a movement two days a week produces the same strength gains as more frequent splits — Grgic et al., Sports Medicine Open.

Stopping short of failure. Strength gains hold up well when sets stop a couple of reps short, and the recovery cost is far lower — Robinson et al., meta-regression.

Long rests on the heavy lifts. Rests beyond two minutes beat short ones for strength, which is why the pull-ups get three minutes and the wrist work gets one — Grgic et al., systematic review.

The finger protocol. Ten-second holds, two to five sets, three minutes rest, stopping about three seconds short of failure, twice weekly with 48 hours between — Eva López's max hang research, summarised here.

The pinch position. Eric Hörst calls wide pinch with the wrist extended the single most overlooked position climbers should train — Training for Climbing.

L-sit ladder and hold times. Feet down, one foot up, tuck, slight extension, one leg, full — GMB's progression.

General training guidance, not medical advice. Sharp pain, swelling, or anything that hasn't settled within a few days is worth a physiotherapist rather than another week of guessing.
